Our Beginnings

The first stitch was made by Mr. José Balada Soler, affectionately known as Don Pepe, who in 1936 decided to leave his job as an accountant at Camisería Barcelona to start his own accounting firm. He was the son of Catalan immigrants who arrived in Chile fleeing the aftermath of World War I. His father, Don Jacinto, was a gifted pianist, but was unable to earn enough through his art to support the family. He therefore had to rely on his work as a teacher and the small business skills of his wife, Doña Dolores Soler, to make ends meet during a time of great scarcity. Many of the families who hired him for piano lessons continue to be BALADA clients to this day, now in their third or even fourth generation.

This hardworking couple managed to ensure that their three children became professionals, but beyond that, they instilled in them the value of independence as a guarantee of security and economic freedom.

That is how we began: as a source of prosperity for the Balada family and as a provider of dignified work for accountants who, at that time in a poor Chile, were often the first in their families to have a formal education. Don Pepe had the foresight to remain active by systematically studying tax specialization, which allowed us to expand both the quantity and quality of our clients. But he did not do it alone; we were fortunate that others took the company's growth as their own mission. Most of them were women accountants who, in recognizing a firm that valued their professional capabilities, found a rare and welcoming workspace at a time when job opportunities for women were scarce.

First years and the life of our founder (1936 to 1960)

A few years after Don Pepe began his independent work, World War II broke out, making earnings scarce, although there was no shortage of work, a situation that remains true to this day. The difficulties that came with this historic moment motivated him to start a business in transport. He bought two "micros" (urban buses), which he signed in the "San Pablo" route, he became a businessman and ended up owning 50 vehicles, with his own workshop and a partner in charge of the whole operation. The business came to an end due to conflicts with said parter, who suffered from delirum tremens.

The accounting company continued to grow silently and systematically. Having begun its activities in a small office in San Antonio street, shortly after it moved to Unión Central street and ended up at Nueva York street, next to the Bolsa de Comercio and Club de la Unión, in other words, the best business district at the time.

The golden age, between 1960 and 1980

In those years there were no computers, everything was analog, workdays were long and the work exhausting, to a point in which during balance closing times the whole team had to sleep at the office for a month. All this generated a sort of mystique that gave Balada a life of its own and transformed the team into a family.

In 1965 when we were one of the main accounting studios of the country, Don Pepe got married to who would end up being one of the most loyal and careful workers, doña María Violeta Valero Garrido, "Señora Violeta". She stood out for her unbreakable tenacity. Balada was her first and only job, she began as an apprentice in 1947, ended up being in charge of the whole office and retired when her first of three children was born, establishing that she would support her husband through the nurturing of their family.

Her father, Don Juan Valero, was a hardworking man, an artist, stained glass designer, whom next to his family also lived in poverty. during the second World War, when commmissions ran out, he had to take refuge in his trade as a glazier and to make a living he fixed windows in ambulances, hospitals and similar establishments. Our seal of prolixity, profesional rigurosity and work dedication, that ms. Violeta first introduced to the company and then instilled in her children, is the importante legacy that we received from Don Juan.

At the beginning of the 70's Don Pepe added to his accounting job, his visionaire businessman gift, becoming a pioneer in the development of applied computation to accounting and administrative processes. Balada hired engineers, systems analysts, imported printers and computers, in order to offer services of data processing to his clients. We moved to three floors at a building in París 720 street.

Success lead him to abandon the audit division to focus in supporting the automatization of administrative tasks and the economic management of the companies he worked with, wihtout leaving accounting and tax advisory. This lead us to have more than 70 hired professionals, gain access to lend services to financial institutions, publicly traded companies and public entities. We experienced an exponential growth. This time it was the entrepreneurship, plus our collaborators, which allowed us to avoid without losses the political, economical and social crisis of those years, one of the worst our country has been through.

Hard times and the entrance of the second generation, from 1980 to 2000.

Every story has hard times and ours began in 1982. We were in the process of importing a computer to attend the increasing demand of computational services that we were facing, when the economical crisis which caused the bankcrupcy of a big part of the national industry broke out, breaking the finantial sytem and shooting up the dollar value. Our debt was in dollars. In order to deal with this difficult situation we had to downsize our computational unit, and absorb it from the accounting area later on, salaries were cut and debt started to raise.

We faced the 90's downsized, impoverished; but economically balanced. In those years Don Pepe was reaching his 75 years of age, he showed certain vitality that hid his age and alzheimer's for years, which acted as a bactery that eated the company away in silence, making us sistematically lose our main clients, in spite of our efforts to keep them, wihtout understanding why.

We found out in 1992, through one of our old clients - from those who we attend through generations -, that our leader showed erratic behavior in meetings and that he would constantly lose track of conversations. This information was received by doña Úrsula Trejo, "Señora Úrsula", for her Balada was also her only job, reaching company partner status thanks to her special gift of softly dealing with people and managing tasks at SII; on top of having an unwavering loyalty, she lended more than 50 years of her life to our company.

At that moment, our conomic situation was critical, we owed taxes, salaries, and there was a huge ammount of work that was already payed for by clients. We had gotten used to economic burden, to a point of not reallizing that the company was actually headless, but the client's call allerted Ms. Úrsula, whom contacted the first son of Don Pepe, José Ignacio Balada Valero, and asked for an urgent meeting, to discuss the situation.

José Ignacio had just finished his Bachelor of Business Administration, but had been working at the company since he turned 16, at first as a junior serving coffee, delivering mail and so on. During the years of economic crisis he led the financial restructuring of the company, renegotiating debts, updating services and modernizing equipment. He went of to finish his studies and gain experience at other companies.

Once he understood the gravity of his father's health and the delicate financial state of the company, aware that it was not consequence of the detriment of the business but the loss of faculties of its leader, the family agreed to entrust José Ignacio with the task of reviving the company. As part of this agreement, they committed to ensuring the necessary cash flow to cover the founder's financial needs during his lifetime, in exchange for transferring ownership of the company to José Ignacio.

With this, the leadership change came to be during march of 1992, at a time where the company was in a situation of total insolvency and in debt with its clients: payed but not donde work, that surpassed financial liabilities. This work had to be served before the 30th of april from said year, with the anual closing of all accounting and the presentation of all declared taxes.

All this forced the new leader to focus on the task at hand, motivating the teams of professionals to work from monday to sunday, long work journeys, for a commitment with a dying company. All tax declarations where delivered, many of them inside the bank, after closing hours, where we would finish our work (there was no SII website, nor online payments). Our deeply rooted family spirit was still alive, it was those people that made possible for Balada to survive the worst economic, operational and leadership crisis in it's history, without knowing if they would be payed.

New beginnings, from 2000 to 2014

It might sound like fantasy, but in the year 2000 we payed our last due debt, which gave us the stregnth needed to move to an ofice at Providencia avenue. It was the beginning of recovering the market position we always had.

With the new millenium began the modernization of the state, affidavits started, computational crossings between the SII and tax reforms, which came with new and complex rules of control, on top of more penalties. All this generated a deep change in our work, which translated in the need to prioritize legal analysis over accounting, for which Balada hired its first associate lawyer and started going into the tax advisory market.

These years where plentiful, we increased our professional personel and patrimonial reorganization started to bloom. With the help of Don Alejandro Dumay Peña, at the time tax manager at the CMPC, today our "counselor" in tax matters, we imprinted the seal of problem solving and empathy with the client that stills makes us stand out in our recommendations in succession and corporate matters.

In the year 2005 we travelled to Switzerland and met the world of Trust funds, foundations and patrimonial structuring diversifying jurisdictional risk. We entered a new industry, unkown in Chile, which forced us to internationally amplify our range of services, we incorporated foreign clients and took leadership of the advisory to investors that needed to use structures from other jurisdictions for their investments.

Bachelet's big tax reform marked the years from 2014 to 2018

And so we entered the second decade of the new millenium as a top tax advisory firm, with the endorsement of our international experience and a highly specialized professional team. We had to face the tax reform of 2014, plus the two complementaries that came afterwards, which came into effect during 2017. These radically changed the priorities of our services and our clients requirements, because, in one hand, the significant growth of tax rates raised tax planning in business to a strategic level and, on the other hand, the loss of freedom due to the new anti-avoidance normative that implemented taxes to companies long term plans. Is at this point that we want to thank Don Hugo Daudet Proust, R.I.P, whom guided us with his judicial tax experience during a change-intensive time.

Social Outbreak and the pandemic

And so, in these recent times, we have been seizing opportunities, reacting to the new tax reforms, implementing the changes in criteria of the SII, facing the pandemic and the social outbreak, plus other situations that have affect us all. All this triggered an internal process within Balada, in which we have been working hard for almost 10 years, preparing ourselves to deal with changes in the geopolitic, social and economic structures of the world. We have renewed our identity, strengthening our core values and modernizing our way of working. Today, we are ready to support and promote a new vision of “Business” and “Entrepreneurs,” one that is committed to Sustainability in a real and tangible way, aligned with our long-standing tradition of dedication and passion for what we do.
