Our History

Eight decades of growth and trust

Balada

Balada Consultores, almost a century old, has demonstrated exceptional resilience, adapting to the most challenging adversities, from economic crisis to technological advancements.

What has consolidated the company is not only its vast experience in the tax and accounting fields, but also the commitment and collaboration of its team, which has remained united and loyal to the values of professionalism and family. Over the years, it has evolved with every challenge, diversifying its services and opening up to new international horizons — always with the goal of offering innovative and empathetic solutions to its clients.

Our Beginnings

The first stitch was made by Mr. José Balada Soler, affectionately known as Don Pepe, who, in 1936, decided to leave his job as an accountant at Camisería Barcelona to start his own accounting firm. He was the son of Catalan immigrants who arrived in Chile fleeing the aftermath of World War I. His father, Don Jacinto, was a gifted pianist, but was unable to earn enough through his art to support the family. He therefore had to rely on his work as a teacher and the small business skills of his wife, Doña Dolores Soler, to make ends meet during a time of great scarcity. Many of the families who hired him for piano lessons continue to be BALADA clients to this day, now in their third or even fourth generation.

This hardworking couple managed to ensure that their three children became professionals, but beyond that, they instilled in them the value of independence as a guarantee of security and economic freedom.

That is how we began: as a source of prosperity for the Balada family and as a provider of dignified work for accountants who, in that poor Chile, were often the first in their families to have a formal education. Don Pepe had the foresight to remain active by systematically studying tax specialization, which allowed us to expand both the quantity and quality of our clients. But he did not do it alone; we were fortunate that others took the company's growth as their own mission. Most of them were female accountants who, in recognizing a firm that valued their professional capabilities, found a rare and welcoming workspace at a time when job opportunities for women were scarce.

First years and the life of our founder (1936 to 1960)

A few years after Don Pepe began his independent work, World War II broke out, making earnings scarce, although there was no shortage of work — a situation that remains true to this day. The difficulties that came with this historic moment motivated him to start a business in transport. He bought two "micros" (urban buses), which he signed to the "San Pablo" route. He became a businessman and ended up owning 50 vehicles, with his own workshop and a partner in charge of the whole operation. The business came to an end due to conflicts with said partner, who suffered from delirium tremens.

The accounting company continued to grow silently and systematically. Having begun its activities in a small office on San Antonio Street, it shortly after moved to Unión Central Street and eventually to Nueva York Street, next to the Bolsa de Comercio and Club de la Unión — in other words, the best business district at the time.

The golden age, between 1960 and 1980

In those years, there were no computers; everything was analog. Workdays were long, and the work exhausting, to the point that during balance closing times, the whole team had to sleep at the office for a month. All this generated a sort of mystique that gave Balada a life of its own and transformed the team into a family.

In 1965, when we were one of the main accounting firms in the country, Don Pepe married who would end up being one of the most loyal and dedicated workers, Doña María Violeta Valero Garrido, "Señora Violeta." She stood out for her unbreakable tenacity. Balada was her first and only job. She began as an apprentice in 1947, ended up being in charge of the whole office, and retired when her first of three children was born, establishing that she would support her husband through the nurturing of their family.

Her father, Don Juan Valero, was a hardworking man and an artist, a stained-glass designer, who, along with his family, also lived in poverty. During the Second World War, when commissions ran out, he had to take refuge in his trade as a glazier, and to make a living, he fixed windows in ambulances, hospitals, and similar establishments. Our seal of prolixity, professional rigor, and work dedication, which Ms. Violeta first introduced to the company and then instilled in her children, is the important legacy that we received from Don Juan.

At the beginning of the '70s, Don Pepe added to his accounting job his visionary businessman gift, becoming a pioneer in the development of applied computation to accounting and administrative processes. Balada hired engineers, systems analysts, imported printers and computers, in order to offer data processing services to his clients. We moved to three floors in a building on Paris 720 Street.

Success led him to abandon the audit division to focus on supporting the automation of administrative tasks and the economic management of the companies he worked with, without leaving accounting and tax advisory. This led us to have more than 70 hired professionals, gain access to lend services to financial institutions, publicly traded companies, and public entities. We experienced exponential growth. This time, it was entrepreneurship, plus our collaborators, that allowed us to avoid losses during the political, economic, and social crisis of those years, one of the worst our country has been through.

Hard times and the entrance of the second generation, from 1980 to 2000.

Every story has hard times, and ours began in 1982. We were in the process of importing a computer to attend to the increasing demand for computational services we were facing when the economic crisis, which caused the bankruptcy of a large part of the national industry, broke out, disrupting the financial system and causing the dollar value to soar. Our debt was in dollars. To deal with this difficult situation, we had to downsize our computational unit and later absorb it into the accounting area. Salaries were cut, and debt began to rise.

We faced the '90s downsized and impoverished, but economically balanced. During those years, Don Pepe was approaching 75 years of age. He showed certain vitality that hid his age and Alzheimer's for years, which acted as a silent factor that slowly ate away at the company, causing us to systematically lose our main clients, despite our efforts to retain them, without understanding why.

In 1992, we found out through one of our old clients—one of those we had been serving across generations—that our leader was showing erratic behavior in meetings and constantly losing track of conversations. This information was received by Doña Úrsula Trejo, "Señora Úrsula." For her, Balada was also her only job, and she reached company partner status thanks to her special gift of dealing with people and managing tasks at the SII (Internal Revenue Service). On top of her unwavering loyalty, she lent more than 50 years of her life to our company.

At that moment, our economic situation was critical. We owed taxes, salaries, and there was a huge amount of work that had already been paid for by clients. We had gotten used to the economic burden, to the point of not realizing that the company was actually headless, but the client's call alerted Ms. Úrsula, who contacted Don Pepe's first son, José Ignacio Balada Valero, and requested an urgent meeting to discuss the situation.

José Ignacio had just finished his Bachelor of Business Administration, but had been working at the company since he turned 16, at first as a junior serving coffee, delivering mail, and so on. During the years of economic crisis, he led the financial restructuring of the company, renegotiating debts, updating services, and modernizing equipment. He went on to finish his studies and gain experience at other companies.

Once he understood the gravity of his father's health and the delicate financial state of the company, aware that it was not a consequence of the detriment of the business but rather the loss of faculties of its leader, the family agreed to entrust José Ignacio with the task of reviving the company. As part of this agreement, they committed to ensuring the necessary cash flow to cover the founder's financial needs during his lifetime, in exchange for transferring ownership of the company to José Ignacio.

Thus, the leadership change occurred in March of 1992, at a time when the company was in total insolvency and in debt with its clients—work paid for but not done—that surpassed the financial liabilities. This work had to be completed before the 30th of April of that year, along with the annual closing of all accounting and the presentation of all declared taxes.

All of this forced the new leader to focus on the task at hand, motivating the teams of professionals to work from Monday to Sunday, long workdays, committed to a dying company. All tax declarations were submitted, many of them inside the bank after closing hours, where we would finish our work (there was no SII website or online payments). Our deeply rooted family spirit was still alive; it was those people who made it possible for Balada to survive the worst economic, operational, and leadership crisis in its history, without knowing if they would be paid.

New beginnings, from 2000 to 2014

It might sound like fantasy, but in the year 2000, we paid off our last due debt, which gave us the strength needed to move to an office on Providencia Avenue. It marked the beginning of the recovery of the market position we had always held.

With the new millennium came the modernization of the state. Affidavits started, computational crossings between the SII (Internal Revenue Service) and tax reforms introduced new and complex rules of control, along with more penalties. All of this generated a profound change in our work, which translated into the need to prioritize legal analysis over accounting. As a result, Balada hired its first associate lawyer and began to venture into the tax advisory market.

These years were plentiful. We increased our professional personnel, and patrimonial reorganization began to thrive. With the help of Don Alejandro Dumay Peña, who at the time was the tax manager at CMPC, and is now our "counselor" in tax matters, we imprinted the seal of problem-solving and empathy with the client that still makes us stand out in our recommendations on succession and corporate matters.

In 2005, we traveled to Switzerland and entered the world of trust funds, foundations, and patrimonial structuring, diversifying jurisdictional risk. We entered a new industry, previously unknown in Chile, which forced us to internationally expand our range of services. We incorporated foreign clients and took leadership in advising investors who needed to use structures from other jurisdictions for their investments.

Bachelet's big tax reform marked the years from 2014 to 2018

And so, we entered the second decade of the new millennium as a top tax advisory firm, with the endorsement of our international experience and a highly specialized professional team. We had to face the 2014 tax reform, along with the two complementary reforms that followed, which came into effect in 2017. These reforms radically changed the priorities of our services and our clients' requirements. On one hand, the significant growth in tax rates elevated tax planning in business to a strategic level, and on the other hand, the loss of freedom due to the new anti-avoidance regulations implemented taxes on companies' long-term plans. It is at this point that we want to thank Don Hugo Daudet Proust, R.I.P., who guided us with his judicial tax experience during this change-intensive time.

Social Outbreak and the pandemic

And so, in these recent times, we have been seizing opportunities, reacting to the new tax reforms, implementing changes in the SII's criteria, facing the pandemic and the social outbreak, along with other situations that have affected us all. All of this triggered an internal process within Balada, in which we have been working diligently for almost 10 years, preparing ourselves to face changes in the geopolitical, social, and economic structures of the world. We have renewed our identity, strengthening our core values and modernizing our way of working. Today, we are ready to support and promote a new vision of 'Business' and 'Entrepreneurs,' one that is committed to Sustainability in a real and tangible way, aligned with our long-standing tradition of dedication and passion for what we do.
